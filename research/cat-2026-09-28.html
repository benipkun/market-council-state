1Verdict

Great business, expensive stock.

Caterpillar's profits are surging on power equipment for AI data centres, and it keeps beating forecasts. But at about 34 times the last year's earnings, the price already assumes that boom lasts for years.

My call: worth watching, not worth chasing. The next real test is the third-quarter report in late October.

5What's driving it

  • Power for AI data centres. Its generators and gas turbines are in short supply as data centres race for electricity. This is now what moves the stock.
  • Fresh order signal (25 Sep). An AI lab agreed to reimburse the developer Atlas for another 283 MW of Caterpillar power equipment: customers are paying up front to secure supply.
  • Core business. Construction and mining machines, with self-driving haul trucks now expanding to two more quarries in Virginia.
  • Dividend. A long record of raising it every year, though the yield is small at this price.

7Bull case vs bear case

Bull case

  • Earnings are accelerating, not just rising: up 73% on a year earlier last quarter.
  • Customers are paying ahead to lock in power equipment, and the order backlog is large.
  • A top-quality industrial with pricing power and a big service business.

Bear case

  • Priced for perfection: about 34 times earnings for a company that usually trades at 15–20 times.
  • It now trades like an AI stock: any sign of slower AI spending hits it.
  • Higher interest rates weigh on construction and mining demand.
  • Insiders sold about $260M of stock over the past year (GuruFocus).

To sit at a more normal 20–25 times the last year's earnings, Caterpillar needs yearly earnings of about $33–41 a share: 38–72% more than the last twelve months.

So the bet is simple: the second-quarter jump has to be the new normal, and then keep growing. If it is, today's price is roughly fair. If that quarter was a peak, the stock has room to fall.

9Fit with your portfolio

  • One share costs about $822, so a small position would be a fractional share.
  • It would be another single-company bet next to your largest holding, not something that spreads your risk. If spreading risk is the goal, a broad fund does that better than any one stock.
  • It fell 23% from its peak in under three months. Size anything so a move like that would not hurt.

10What to watch next

  • Third-quarter results, late October. The date is not in my data yet; last year's report came on 29 Oct.
  • Power-generation orders and backlog. Still growing, or levelling off?
  • Guidance for next year. Does management expect the data-centre demand to continue?
  • AI spending headlines from the big cloud companies, and long-term interest rates.
